### Added

- **A card says who it rests on by name.** Handing a to-do to somebody has been possible for
  a while, and the board then read »Zuständig: 0199c0de-…« — an identifier that names nobody,
  in the one place on a card a person has to be able to read at a glance. It now says what
  they are called.

  Who gets told is a decision and not a convenience: a name is more identifying than an
  identifier, and a board is among the places this wiki is most careful about, because a card
  already says that a page exists and what somebody wrote on it. Reading the list of accounts
  is an administrator's privilege and stays one — but a card is not that list. It names one
  person, put there deliberately by somebody who may write the page, and only ever somebody
  who may **read** that page; so whoever is looking at the card and whoever is named on it are
  both readers of the same page. That is already the rule a page's history follows, where
  everyone who may read the page is told who published each version.

  Which means the name comes off again. Take somebody's access to the page away, or suspend
  their account, and every board stops naming them the next time it is read — without anybody
  having to go and tidy up the cards. The card still rests on them by identifier, so the
  assignment can still be cleared, which is the whole point of being able to clear it. A card
  that names nobody is never an answer to "is there an account here": it says the same thing
  about a person who may no longer read the page as about one who can no longer sign in, and
  a card can only ever rest on an account that really exists in the first place.

  Who is told, and what a card says when it may not name anybody, is
  [ADR 0009](docs/decisions/0009-who-may-learn-a-board-card-s-assignee.md). Working the names
  out costs one question per person and page rather than one per card, so a board of forty
  cards resting on four people asks four times.
