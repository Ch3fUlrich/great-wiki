### Changed

- **A page you may not read now looks exactly like a page that is not there.** Ask for one —
  the page itself, its backlinks, its version history, its files, its to-dos, its topics, its
  board, or the editor — and the answer is the same "there is no page at this address" you get
  for an address nobody has ever used, down to the last byte. It used to answer *"this page
  exists and is not yours"* for the first and *"there is no such page"* for the second, which
  is one difference you can ask about any address you can type: somebody with an account but
  no access could work out which addresses hold a page and which do not, and go on asking
  until they had mapped the wiki. Addresses are ordinary words and easy to guess; the titles
  behind them are neither, and a title is one grant away from an address known to hold
  something. For a wiki holding a family's medical notes that is worth more than the
  convenience it cost, and the convenience was only ever the owner's: that difference was
  decided when this wiki had exactly one person in it, and the invitation flow has now put a
  second one there.
- **Whoever administers an area still sees what is wrong.** Nothing is hidden from them,
  because they can read the pages in it — a grant that was set up wrongly shows up as "I can
  see this page and the person I gave it to cannot", in the panel that can put it right, which
  is a plainer thing to be told than a status code ever was. And somebody who **can** read a
  page but may not change it is still told that plainly when they try: "you may not do this
  here" rather than "there is nothing here", because they are looking straight at it.
